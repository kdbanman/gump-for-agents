Pulses write a count into a wire, and it stays. A pair of pulses on a second wire laid across it calls the count back out as exactly that many pulses, and another store catches them. The calling pair is still made by hand, and nothing yet copies or subtracts.

The store is a wire B between two caps: 38 + 5N cells for N units, standing for ever with period 2. Each unit of it is read as a letter: 7 is plain wire, d a unit of the variant wire, f the unit at the end of a stretch. A stretch d…df with k letters d holds the number k. A pulse is sent by removing one cell of the wire by hand; two pulses that meet write 1, and each later pulse that reaches the stretch adds one. The call line is a capped wire C of 26 units, 162 cells, laid across the store wire one unit before the stretch’s wall; two cells made live on it at one generation become the pair of pulses that calls.
