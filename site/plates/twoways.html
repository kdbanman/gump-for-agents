A wrapped face of 63 cells dies by over-filling: in one generation it fills to of its area, and the next generation it crashes, its cells dying of overcrowding. A hull face of the same area fades over about ten generations. The difference is the hull face’s rim row, whose cells have fewer neighbours inside the box and survive an over-full generation twice as often.

Two kinds of face, both of 63 cells. A wrapped face is the face layer of a slab closed on itself like a torus, (1,4),(0,21): it has no rim, and every cell of it has 9 neighbours inside the box. A hull face is one of the eight faces of a closed octahedron of radius 11, the kind of face in the box of “The staircase”: its 30 cells along the edges, the rim row, have 7 neighbours inside, its 30 interior cells 9, its 3 corners 4. The launch is the last generation in which at least half the face is alive. The numbers come from an audit with its own code, built from the reference stepper, of another session’s night of work; the other plates of this page are about hull faces only.
