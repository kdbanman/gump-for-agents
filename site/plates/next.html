What is not settled, and what is running.

  1. Can gump compute? It has wires, pulses, a gate where two pulses meet, a counter, a clock of any length, a gun that writes onto a wire, a clock that edits what was written, and an end that answers a gap with an echo sent back up the stream. It has nothing that listens: no object left in one state by a gap and another by its absence, among every end searched and thousands of placements sampled. No corner, no crossing, no second stage. Running now, a sixth push: what an echo and a gap do when they meet on the wire, and whether a gun can survive an echo. running
  2. A clock whose period divides by four. Every built clock has period 4N + 2. Half the reason is found: the remainder belongs to the two ends, and every end that fits inside the wire’s tube has the same one. Ends of the other kind exist one size up, and each destroys itself turning the shuttle round. Why the class is forced is not known. open
  3. Why a way round costs what it does. A living face is a little emptier for every way round it, by an amount that falls smoothly as the way round lengthens, and a tube’s discount in price is 1.85 times that emptiness. Why 1.85 is not known, nor what carries the emptiness round the loop. Whether a loop of √21 costs anything at all: the face that tests it is queued for your card, and so are the tubes of 96 and 108 cells that say whether a tube’s line bends. queued
  4. One price per cell, or two. Faces without a rim say 0.2087 from 63 cells up and show no drift (a face with no loop at all would pay about 0.2075, if the rule for loops holds). Faces with a rim say more, and their local slope is moving away. Your card is running the large rimmed face that would tell. running
  5. Why the thinnest face costs half. Same rule, same five layers of depth, half the price. Nothing explains the ratio. open
  6. What a face remembers. Copies of one state, each with a single cell-orbit flipped, live a little longer when the original did. Something slow in the state sets its odds by a fifth or so, and nobody knows what. seen once, weakly
  7. One sixth. Whether a symmetric face’s cost tends to exactly one sixth of an ordinary face’s as it grows. The data allow it and do not yet demand it. open
