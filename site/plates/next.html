What is not settled, and what is running.

  1. Can gump compute? It has wires, pulses, a gate where two pulses meet, a counter, a clock of any length, a gun that writes onto a wire and a clock that edits what was written. It has nothing that reads: no object left in one state by a gap and another by its absence. No corner, no crossing, no second stage. Asked next: a reader; a way to move the divider’s clock without destroying it; a divide-by-four; a corner as an exact search. open
  2. A clock whose period divides by four. Every built clock has period 4N + 2. Half the reason is found: the remainder belongs to the two ends, and every end that fits inside the wire’s tube has the same one. Ends of the other kind exist one size up, and each destroys itself turning the shuttle round. Why the class is forced is not known. open
  3. Why a face stops seeing itself. A wrapped face pays for every way round shorter than √21 and nothing for the next length up; what sets that length is not known, nor why a tube’s price per cell falls by just 2.0, 1.3 and 0.7 percent as its girth tightens, nor how the discounts of two short ways round combine. Whether a tube’s line bends at all: the faces of 96 and 108 cells are queued for your card. queued
  4. One price per cell, or two. Faces without a rim say 0.2087 from 63 cells up and show no drift. Faces with a rim say more, and their local slope is moving away. Your card is running the large rimmed face that would tell. running
  5. Why the thinnest face costs half. Same rule, same five layers of depth, half the price. Nothing explains the ratio. open
  6. What a face remembers. Copies of one state, each with a single cell-orbit flipped, live a little longer when the original did. Something slow in the state sets its odds by a fifth or so, and nobody knows what. seen once, weakly
  7. One sixth. Whether a symmetric face’s cost tends to exactly one sixth of an ordinary face’s as it grows. The data allow it and do not yet demand it. open
