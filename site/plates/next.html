What is not settled, and what is running.

  1. Can gump compute? It has wires, pulses, a gate where two pulses meet, a counter, a clock of any length, a gun that writes onto a wire, a clock that edits what was written, an end that answers a gap with an echo sent back up the stream, and a stream that carries nine known kinds of signal, three going up and six down. It has nothing that listens: no object left in one lasting state by a signal and another by its absence. Signals going opposite ways do not pass, and no gun survives an echo. Open now: the full meeting table of the nine signals (the five found last are untested against the rest) and whether there are more; a count that lasts (an echo against several holes writes one unit per hole, but only inside a stream being destroyed: is there anything that stops the spreading edges and stands?); and a way for the gun to survive being answered (eleven guns absorb the one-cell up-signal U, and U cancels K0, so an end that answered with U instead of an echo would give a wire that survives its answer; none inside the tube, beside it untried). The ledger: research/OPEN-PROBLEMS.md, item 13, questions (q) to (t). open
  2. A clock whose period divides by four. Every built clock has period 4N + 2. Half the reason is found: the remainder belongs to the two ends, and every end that fits inside the wire’s tube has the same one. Ends of the other kind exist one size up, and each destroys itself turning the shuttle round. Why the class is forced is not known. open
  3. How each kind of face dies. There is no single price per cell: a face with a rim pays about 0.231, a wide wrapped face 0.2087, a tube less, a long tube less again. The difference is not in the living face: deep inside, a rimmed face is as full as a wrapped one, and how much chaos lies behind it changes nothing. What a rimmed face has that a wrapped one lacks is nearly empty corners and one fuller row along each edge. Nobody has yet watched the two kinds die side by side: where the last living patch sits, whether a rimmed face’s death must start away from its corners or at them. The ledger: research/OPEN-PROBLEMS.md, item 9 (k). open
  4. Why a way round costs what it does. A living face is a little emptier for every way round it, by an amount that falls smoothly as the way round lengthens. On tubes up to 84 cells the discount in price was 1.85 times that emptiness; tubes of girth √12 at 96 and 108 cells live shorter than their line said, by about five standard errors, and would need 3.0. Open: whether a long tube bends for ever or follows a second straight line, and whether tubes of girth √13 and 4 bend too (9 (l)); whether a loop of √21 costs anything at all (your card’s face came in between the two forecasts: 0.0047 ± 0.0023, a 2σ answer; a third such face would settle it, 9 (m)). open
  5. Why the thinnest face costs half. Same rule, same five layers of depth, half the price. Nothing explains the ratio. open
  6. What a face remembers. Copies of one state, each with a single cell-orbit flipped, live a little longer when the original did. Something slow in the state sets its odds by a fifth or so, and nobody knows what. seen once, weakly
  7. One sixth. Whether a symmetric face’s cost tends to exactly one sixth of an ordinary face’s as it grows. The data allow it and do not yet demand it. open
