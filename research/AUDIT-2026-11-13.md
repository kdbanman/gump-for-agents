# Audit, 2026-11-13

An independent audit of the project's claims (auditor agent, one core, nothing longer than ten minutes). Companion
files: `research/CLAIMS.md` (ledger of 71 headline claims with verification levels), `research/check.sh` (standing
checks, also run by `.github/workflows/check.yml`), and the auditor's own code in `research/audit/`:
`independent.py` (rule and theorems rebuilt from face geometry, plus an engine cross-check), `clockwork_check.py`,
`data_claims.py` (headline numbers re-derived from committed data). Bulk output of the re-runs is in
`research/scratch/audit/` (not in git).

I edited none of NOTEBOOK, STATUS, README, WHAT-I-SEE, the tour, AUTOMATON, OPEN-PROBLEMS or anyone's scripts.
Each finding ends with a suggested fix for the lead.

## Summary

- **The foundations hold.** T1–T4, the twist lemma, the threshold lemma (S5) and the sealed-set theorem (S6,
  Theorem 3) survive a from-scratch re-derivation. The engine, the oracle, the original's logs and the other steppers
  (numpy, `rep.c`, `fl.c`, and mine) agree bit for bit. Every exact number I could recompute (cube counts, hull sizes, face areas, the
  vertex star, the clockwork periods and twists, still-life counts to n = 13, p2 counts to union 11, the side-11/13/14/15
  schedules, the side-16 schedule from its event log) is right.
- **Re-run statistics agree within their errors**: bulk density, χ, the density autocorrelation, whole-torus
  lifetimes, the face end-game at R = 9, the B45/S3457 medians.
- **The most serious problem is one of method.** The "cubes are over-dispersed, P ≈ 0.01" result counts the side-16
  plateaus that suggested the hypothesis in the first place. Without them the P-value is about 0.18 (finding 1).
- Several smaller errors and inconsistencies follow: one wrong statement in the tour, a stale density in the notebook,
  a curated table row that does not reproduce, a "correction" that blames the wrong cause, numbers that differ from
  the script that is supposed to print them, and validators that report a mismatch but still exit 0.

### Unsupported

**U1. The over-dispersion of cube lineages (P ≈ 0.01) is a post-hoc test.** Quoted in NOTEBOOK 11-13 (P = 0.003 and
0.008, then 0.0029/0.0082), STATUS ("P ≈ 0.01"), OPEN-PROBLEMS 6, and WHAT-I-SEE ("one chance in a hundred").
The arithmetic is correct: `cube_vs_ensemble.py` re-runs, the 16 ratios match, and P(Γ₁₆ ≥ 27.2) = 0.0080. But:
1. The hypothesis was formed because side 16 outlived its copies. Its six plateaus contribute 14.4 of the 27.2.
   **Without side 16**, the ten plateaus of sides 13–15 sum to 12.8 against 10 expected: P(Γ₁₀ ≥ 12.8) = **0.18**.
   The data that generated the hypothesis cannot also test it.
2. The thresholds (> 4 means, < 0.05 means) and the cut A ≥ 408 were chosen after looking. Testing "both tails" makes
   this worse.
3. The τ in each ratio is itself estimated, from 20–167 deaths at A ≥ 570 (±8–22% in τ). That error is not carried
   into the test. At R = 40 the reference τ pools the cube's own clones (96 of its 142 deaths).

So "measured → conjecture" is the right tag, but the P-values should not be quoted as evidence.
*Fix:* report P without side 16 (≈0.18). State that the question is open and untested on independent data.
Pre-register the test, with thresholds and the reference ensembles fixed in advance, before the lineage study or a
side-17 run produces new plateaus.

**U2. "~10⁴¹" is an extrapolation but is tagged measured.** README 5, STATUS 4, WHAT-I-SEE, and the tour (tagged
`measured`, with "(law, extrapolated)" after the tag). It applies the asymmetric face law, measured for A ≤ 90 (one
peel at 108), to A = 459. Whether 0.208 per site holds beyond A = 84 is listed as open in OPEN-PROBLEMS 9 and the
fluctuation README. The arithmetic is right (exp(−0.09 + 0.209·459) = 4×10⁴¹).
*Fix:* tag it conjecture in the tour, and say "on the face law, extrapolated fivefold in area" wherever it appears.

**U3. The side-19 "beyond 10¹²" has nothing to reproduce it from.** WHAT-I-SEE and the tour say "beyond 10¹²", the
NOTEBOOK says "10¹³–10¹⁴ on these fits, far outside any data". No command or table carries the A = 1053 extrapolation
(level D). *Fix:* add one line to `sym_fit.py` that prints it, and use one range everywhere.

**U4. The face-law constants have no fitting script.** `exp(−0.09 + 0.209·A)` and its errors are typed in by hand
(`research/peeling/figures.py`: `C_FACE, A_FACE = 0.2088, -0.090  # slab fit (NOTEBOOK)`). The constants can be
re-derived from `slab_area.csv`: with the figure's selection and inverse-variance weights I get −0.091 + 0.2088·A,
max residual 0.18. The stated errors (±0.06, ±0.002) are about 1.3–2× the weighted-fit errors (±0.047, ±0.0009), so
they are conservative and acceptable. The *selection* of batches, though, is only implicit.
*Fix:* commit the fit (it is in `research/audit/data_claims.py`, check `facelaw`; move it next to the table).

**U5. The random-map heuristic is not derived.** OPEN-PROBLEMS 4 and PROGRAM E2 say "about τ of the 2^N hull states
lie on immortal cycles; a seed reaches one with probability ~τ²/2^N". No derivation is given. For a uniformly random
map on M states, about √(πM/2) states are cyclic, so the τ-scaling needs a stated model. It is tagged heuristic and
says "not checked numerically". *Fix:* write out the model, or tag it as a question rather than an estimate.

## Proofs (part b)

- **T1–T4, axis half-spaces, speed limit.** I rebuilt the neighbourhood from face geometry: faces as sets of four
  corner vertices, neighbours = perpendicular faces sharing two vertices plus coplanar faces sharing exactly one.
  This matches AUTOMATON §2. I then checked T1 for all 3 orientations × 8 signed diagonals. T2: at most 3 neighbours
  across one dead layer, at most 1 across two. T3: the middle of three dead layers sees at most 2, the edges at most
  3, and a cell in a two-layer gap can see 4. T4: 6 in-layer neighbours, every cell on exactly two in-layer
  triangles, which is the kagome net. The proofs in AUTOMATON are complete as written. I tried and failed to break
  T3 with the "Δs = ±4 from both sides" case: 1 + 1 = 2 < 4.
- **Twist lemma.** The proof holds: k(gh) = k(g) + k(h) because G commutes with F. The kernel is Stab(x₀) and the
  image is cyclic. m divides the order of an element of O_h, so m ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6}. A g-fixed cell repeats after L
  steps because F^{t+L}x₀ = g·F^t x₀. `clockwork_check.py` confirms P, L, the twist and the per-cell periods for
  P66.27a, P48.34a, C48.24a, C36.33a, C12.43a, C40.24a, P15.22a and C35.29a, and that C44.26a has no cell of period 44.
- **Threshold lemma (S5).** I re-derived the identity ΔE = −Σ(x_i(t+1) − x_i(t−1))(h_i(t) − 3.5). It needs W
  symmetric, the diagonal included. I checked the case split: the rule equals the uncapped one exactly at cells that
  are not overcrowded. I checked the bounds −6N ≤ E ≤ 7N, hence at most 26N mismatches for U. The per-period counting
  (2R₃ ≤ ΣM ≤ 20·ΣO1) holds: a non-constant cyclic 0/1 sequence changes an even, non-zero number of times, and the
  drive per O1 event is at most 9.5. The audit note about ordered pairs is right. One wording gap: PROGRAM S5 still
  says "(a) no overcrowded cell" where threshold.md proves the weaker hypothesis "no O1 event". Both are true.
- **Sealed sets (S6, Theorem 3).** Checked: the identity e + 2(a + b) = 4d for the normal-x face; the extremal-cell
  argument (3 in-layer + at least 1 of a, b, e outside ⇒ out ≥ 4 when m ≥ 3); the lift to the torus; the hull case.
  Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 are correct. One caveat: the torus lift assumes the torus is large enough for neighbourhoods
  not to overlap. On 1–2-cube-wide tori the graph is a multigraph, which the draft mentions elsewhere.
- **Not attempted:** Eppstein's relation to T2 (OPEN-PROBLEMS 16); the minimality SAT checks of Theorem 4 and
  Proposition 5.

## Statistical method for what I could not re-run (part d)

I checked that the stated errors follow from the stated counts:
- **Exponential means, ± mean/√k:** R = 9 (2300 ± 6 from 137,243 peels), R = 11 (± 816 from 16,240), R = 13
  (± 4.8×10⁵ from 342), the lead's 221- and 118-run samples. All correct.
- **ln τ ± 1/√deaths** in the symmetric table: all rows correct.
- **95% χ² intervals** in `slab_area.csv` (checked the 3×3, m = 1 row exactly) and the 90% interval at R = 42
  (45 deaths → 5.6–9.2×10⁸): correct.
- **Single-wait interval** [T/3.7, T/0.025]: correct.
- **Tail probabilities:** e^{−7.4} = 6×10⁻⁴; e^{−11.8} = 7×10⁻⁶; e^{−3.3} = 0.036; Poisson P(≥ 3 | 0.29) = 0.003;
  the Γ tail sums. All correct.
- **Differences in σ:** ln 0.70 ± 0.20 ("1.5σ above 1.5"); copies vs soups ln 0.42 ± 0.25 (1.7σ); 1.8σ (fluct
  correction 2). All correct.
- **The 759 single-death interval** "2×10⁹ … 1.2×10¹¹": for one event in a fixed exposure the 90% lower end is
  E/4.74 = 1.4×10⁹. The stated lower end uses the 2-degrees-of-freedom quantile on both sides. This is minor.

The error bars are honest. The weak points are **selection** (U1), **errors in the reference τ not propagated**
(U1, item 3), and **protocol details not recorded with the number** (W3, I1). For example, what counts as
"established" at t0 changes the R = 9 mean by 50%.

## Standing checks (part e): `research/check.sh`

`research/check.sh` prints one line per check and exits non-zero on any failure. It runs in 27 s on one core once
the engine is built, about 100 s in a fresh clone with a single-job build. It passes (20/20; 24/24 with `--long` and
`LEGACY_DIR`). It covers:
- engine build, `cargo test` (27 tests), engine vs the four golden logs (open space and legacy torus);
- engine vs the oracle (5 rules, tori and open space), packed ensembles vs per-generation runs, ensemble layer
  deaths vs `--events`;
- oracle vs golden logs for sides 8–10;
- T1–T4 twice: the reference brute force, and the auditor's from-geometry rebuild with the engine cross-check;
- clockwork periods and twists; the threshold-lemma checks and the energy-identity self-test;
- fl.c, rep.c and gl.py vs the engine; the headline numbers from committed data (`data_claims.py`);
- the side-11 and side-13 peel schedules and end states.

With `--long` it adds the side-11 oracle run and the side-14/15 lifetimes, about 6 minutes. With
`LEGACY_DIR=/path/to/kdbanman/gump/dev/cubic_init_results` it adds the side-12/13 legacy logs.

`.github/workflows/check.yml` runs `bash research/check.sh` on pull requests, on pushes to main, and on manual
dispatch: ubuntu-latest, stable Rust, Python 3.12 with numpy. **Untested**: I cannot run Actions here. What I did
test is a fresh `git clone` of this repository run through `check.sh`, which is what the workflow does. Risks:
`.cargo/config.toml` builds with `target-cpu=native` (fine on a runner; the AVX-512 path is optional); the runner's
gcc builds `fl.c` and `rep.c` with `-march=native` at run time. The workflow does not run the web tests (they need
Playwright and Chromium), the `--long` checks, or the side-12/13 logs (those live in the original repository).

**For future work:** when a claim becomes an exact, fast anchor, add one line to `check.sh` and one row to
`CLAIMS.md`. Statistical claims belong in `CLAIMS.md` with their command, not in `check.sh`.
